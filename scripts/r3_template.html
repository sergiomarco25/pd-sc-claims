Report R3 · internal replication · 22 Oct 2026

Do Parkinson's single-cell claims hold up in their own data?

Each claim re-tested in the dataset it came from, with one shared pipeline: donors (donor × region) as the unit, pseudobulk edgeR for genes, log-ratio proportions for composition, co-expression and donor spread for cell states. This is the baseline before testing every claim across the other datasets.

How to read the outcomes
reproducedSignificant in the claimed direction (gene sets: set test p < 0.05; single genes: FDR < 0.05).
direction onlyClaimed direction, not significant with donors as the unit; or a coherent state seen in fewer than half the donors.
not reproducedOpposite direction, not significant.
contradictedSignificant in the opposite direction.

Patterns

Composition claims fail most; gene-level claims mostly survive their own data

Gene-level claims are re-tested on gene lists the authors selected in this same data, so a high internal rate is expected and partly circular; the cross-dataset test (next report) is the real check. Composition claims have no such advantage, and most lose significance once each donor counts once.

What I need you to check

1. Outcome rules

Are the four outcome definitions above what you want to report? In particular: should "direction only" count as partial support, and is "seen in ≥ 50% of donors" the right bar for a cell state?

2. Next: cross-dataset replication (Phase 5)

Every testable claim × every other dataset that has the cell type and groups, reported for the same region and other regions, plus a random-effects meta-analysis. The DE cache and test engine are already built for it.

3. The remaining claim types

GWAS enrichment needs PD GWAS summary statistics (proposal: the public Nalls 2019 meta-analysis, MAGMA gene-level); communication claims need LIANA per dataset; trajectory, chromatin and spatial claims are mostly testable only in their own data. Proposal: do GWAS and communication in Phase 5, report the rest as "not testable elsewhere".
