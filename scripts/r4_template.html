Which Parkinson's single-cell claims hold up in other people's data?

Every claim tested in every other dataset that has the cell type and donor groups it needs, with the same donor-level tests as R3, then combined across datasets. A claim counts as robust when the combined evidence supports it and at least half of the datasets reproduce it on their own.

How to read robustness
robust≥ 3 datasets, combined p < 0.05 as claimed, and ≥ 50% reproduce it individually.
supportedCombined p < 0.05 as claimed, but fewer than half reproduce it individually (or only 2 datasets).
inconsistentNot significant combined, and datasets disagree strongly (I² ≥ 50%).
not replicatedNot significant combined, datasets agree there is little effect.
contradictedCombined p < 0.05 in the opposite direction.
insufficientTestable in fewer than 2 other datasets.
Two rules worth knowing. (1) A cell-state claim counts as reproduced only if the state's markers co-vary beyond random genes matched for expression and parent-cell-type specificity (after removing sequencing-depth effects), a distinct high-scoring tail of nuclei exists (at least twice what random sets give), and at least half the donors contribute; a coherent but continuous programme is "direction only". (2) The four "no difference" claims are scored with an equivalence-style rule: reproduced where no significant difference is found. With few donors this rule is lenient, so read their "robust" calls with that in mind.
